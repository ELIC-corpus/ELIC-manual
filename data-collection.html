ELIC corpus: Procedures and guidelines

Chapter 1 Data collection

Interviews should be conducted by native speakers of the individual variety or a similar variety whenever possible. All portions of the interview should be recorded.

1.1 Recruiting speakers

For this research we are seeking fluent and active speakers of the individual varieties being studied. If possible, you should try to recruit speakers of different ages, both male and female. Our goal is to document the varieties as they are spoken today, so you should not focus solely on the oldest generation, who may be considered to speak the “purest” version of the given variety. Speakers may code-switch during the interview; this is to be expected, and this does not disqualify them from participation.

When inviting individuals to participate in this project, you may say something like the following:

Pozivamo vas na sudjelovanje u projektu Ugroženi jezici u dodiru u Istri i Kvarneru: Višejezični govorni korpus za jezično dokumentiranje i istraživanje/Endangered languages in contact in Istria and Kvarner, Croatia: A multilingual spoken corpus for language documentation and research. Cilj razgovora s Vama je prikupljanje govorne građe u svrhu dokumentiranja, očuvanja i znanstvenog proučavanja ______ (ime jezičnog varijeteta). Razgovor će se voditi vezano za teme iz svakodnevnog života i neka Vaša životna iskustva, a na kraju razgovora ispunit ćemo zajedno kratki demografski upitnik. Intervju će trajati najviše devedesetak minuta. Razgovor će biti anoniman a audiosnimka razgovora će biti pohranjenja u buduću jezičnu arhivu projekta. Ova će se audiosnimka moći koristiti isključivo u znanstvene svrhe a vaše sudjelovanje je dobrovoljno. Ukoliko vas zanima, možete dobiti snimku intervjua.

1.2 Materials needed

You will need the following materials for the interview:

  1. Digital recorder (provided by the principal investigators). Make sure that the recorder is fully charged and that you have the charger with you (or spare batteries, if applicable).
  2. Oral consent form (which you will read and fill out)
  3. GDPR consent, no signature (which you will give to the speaker after they provide their oral consent to participate)
  4. Sociolinguistic interview guide (which you will use to conduct the interview)
  5. Demographic questionnaire (which you will fill out with the participant at the end of the interview and later enter the answers into the Google form)

These documents are available in the folders labeled “Consent documents” and “Data collection documents” in the shared OneDrive folder.

1.2.1 Sociolinguistic interview guide

The questions below are suggestions for topics; the goal of the sociolinguistic interview is to elicit paragraph-length examples of casual speech, and the actual content of the interview is less important. Certain types of questions (e.g., about unusual or frightening experiences) are considered to be especially good for getting the speaker caught up in the story, so that they speak in a more typical manner without consciously monitoring their own language use, but the interviewer should never push anyone to talk about something that they do not want to discuss. It is desirable to include questions about the way of life, customs, and events in the local area, since these are also likely to favor the use of specifically local linguistic variants, but the interviewer should follow the lead of the participants and allow them to talk about subjects that are of interest to them. The only obligatory portion of the interview is the section about language usage and attitudes.

(A Croatian version of the sociolinguistic interview guide is provided to interviewers.)

  1. Childhood
  • Where were you born and were did you grow up?
  • What are your favorite memories from childhood? Are there any events that you particularly remember? Tell me more about that.
  • Did you move? If so, where, and how long did you live in each place? If you moved as a child, do you remember moving? Tell me about that.
  • Did you visit your grandparents? Did you enjoy visiting them? What would you do there?
  • Do you have any brothers or sisters? What was it like growing up with them?
  • How did children play when you were a child?
  • Were you a mischievous child?
  • What do you think about how children grow up today, especially in comparison with how you grew up?
  1. Family
  • Are your parents from your hometown? (if a larger city: what part of town did they grow up in?) If not, where are they from? What about your grandparents?
  • How did you speak at home? (what language/dialect?) And with your grandparents?
  • What celebrations or holidays did you have in your family? What was/is your favorite family holiday/celebration? Why?
  • Many people have a favorite aunt, uncle, or other family member. Did/do you have someone like that in your family? What makes your relationship with them special?
  • Are there any stories that people in your family like to tell about the family, for example, funny stories about things that happened in the past?
  • Are there any special customs or traditions in your family connected with food? What is your favorite family dish? Why?
  1. City/town
  • Tell me about the place where you live (village, small town, neighborhood in larger city).
  • What are your neighbors like? What kind of relationships do you have with them? What are people in your city/region like? What do they like to do? What do they like to talk about?
  • Has your village/town/neighborhood changed since you’ve been living there? How? Why?
  • What do you like best about where you live? What do you not like about it?
  • Is the place where you live important to you? Do you have a sense of belonging to it?
  1. Education
  • Where did you go to elementary / high school?
  • Did you like school? Which did you like better, elementary or high school? Why? Can you tell me about any favorite memories of school?
  • Did you have a favorite teacher? Or a teacher that you particularly didn’t like? Why? Tell me about them.
  • Were you ever treated unfairly, or accused of something that you didn’t do?
  • What were the other students like? Were there different groups of people who were friends, were there any particular cliques that you remember?
  • Who were your best friends? Did you stay friends with them?
  • (Ask about post-secondary education if relevant.)
  1. Friends and free time
  • Do you have a lot of friends? What kinds of things do you do with them?
  • Are most of your friends from childhood / school? If so, why do you think you’re still friends? What do you expect out of a friendship / what’s most important to you in a friend?
  • What’s the craziest/funniest thing that you’ve ever done with your friends?
  • Do you and your friends participate in any local groups / clubs / festivals? Tell me about this.
  • What’s your favorite thing to do in your free time? How do you relax?
  1. Travel
  • Did you travel/go on vacation with your family when you were a child? What was your favorite trip? Please tell me about it.
  • Do you travel now as an adult? Where do you go, and with whom? What has been your favorite trip? What are your best memories from trips that you’ve taken?
  • Do you like to travel? What do you like about it?
  • What is the funniest / strangest / worst experience you’ve had when traveling?
  1. Music, sports, hobbies
  • What music do you like to listen to? What’s your favorite group / performer? When do you listen to music? Do you go to concerts?
  • Do you play an instrument or sing? (or did you do this when you were younger?)
  • What was your first favorite group / performer when you were young? Describe the first concert that you went to. What was the first album that you bought?
  • Do you play any sports? Are you a sports fan? What’s your favorite sport / team? Is playing a sport / rooting for a team important to you? Why?
  • Do you have any hobbies? What do you like about them?
  • Do you watch a lot of television or movies? What is your favorite film / TV show? Why?
  1. Work
  • What did you want to be when you were growing up?
  • What do you do now? Would you like to continue in this job / profession, or do you dream about doing something different? What attracts you to this job?
  • Are you one of those people who live to work, or who work to live? Why?
  • What kinds of things have you learned in your work experience?
  1. Unusual experiences
  • When people talk about their lives, they usually remember something that for them was an unusual experience. Do you have any stories like that? (e.g., a particularly lucky or unlucky experience, something that really scared or surprised you, a strange dream, etc.)
  • Have you ever had an experience where you were in fear for your life? Why? What happened?
  • Are you superstitious? Do you have any particular things that you do, rituals that you follow that are connected with this?
  • Do you or does anyone you know believe in the supernatural? What are their/your beliefs?
  • Have you ever had a dream that later came true?
  1. Significant events
  • Are there any events that have been especially important for the place where you live (city / town / country)? How did they change your life or the lives of other people?
  • (Ask about specific events that may be relevant.)
  1. Language (obligatory)
  • How do people in your area talk? Are there certain features about how they speak that allow other people to recognize where they’re from? Do you easily recognize where other people are from, based on how they speak?
  • Do you speak in different ways, based on the situation or the people that you’re speaking with? (e.g., standard Croatian versus other languages/varieties)
  • Do you think you sound like a typical person from your area? Why or why not? Do you like it when people know where you’re from based on how you speak? Do you think it’s important to preserve local varieties?
  • Has anyone ever criticized you for the way you speak or pointed out something about the way you talk? What did they say? What was your reaction to this?
  • Do you think you talk like your parents? In what way do you speak similarly or differently?
  • Have you ever consciously tried to change the way you speak? Why? How did that go?

1.2.2 Demographic questionnaire

At the end of the interview, please collect the following information about the participant. (A form in Croatian is provided to interviewers). After the interview, please enter this information into the provided Google form.

  1. Date of birth
  2. Place of birth
  3. Gender
  4. Place of residence
  5. Places lived during childhood (up to 18 years of age) and approximate length of time in each
  6. Who did the participant live with as a child? (e.g., both parents, mother, father, grandparent(s))
  7. Languages/dialects used in the home during childhood
  8. Which language/dialect was the primary one used at home, if there was more than one?
  9. Where did the participant’s mother grow up? (adapt as needed based on the answer to question 6)
  10. What is the first language/dialect of the participant’s mother? (adapt as needed based on the answer to question 6)
  11. Where did the participant’s father grow up? (adapt as needed based on the answer to question 6)
  12. What is the first language/dialect of the participant’s father? (adapt as needed based on the answer to question 6)
  13. Elementary and secondary education (list schools and grades completed there)
  14. To which ethnic group did most of the participant’s friends and other peers belong in school?
  15. Post-secondary education (years completed/degrees earned, institutions)
  16. Places lived as an adult (18+) and approximate length of time in each
  17. Current occupation
  18. How does the participant identify (e.g., as Croatian, Istrian, inhabitant of Rijeka, etc.). More than one answer is possible.
  19. Languages/dialects used in everyday life (e.g., at home, at work, with friends)

1.3 Before the interview

  • Read all of the documents carefully, especially the sociolinguistic interview guide, so that the interview will go smoothly.

  • Familiarize yourself with using the recorder before you conduct the interview. Interviews should be recorded in mono, rather than stereo (which is often the default setting on recorders). The recording format should be: .wav, 44.1 kHz sampling rate, 16-bit resolution. Verify these settings prior to the interview. Practice making some short recordings and setting the recording level appropriately. (Ask one of the principal investigators how to do this if you’re not sure.) Make sure that enough space is available on the memory card. A 90-minute interview recorded with these settings will require approximately 1.5 GB.

  • Determine the code to be used for the speaker (you will fill this in on the oral consent form during the interview). Speaker codes consist of a 3-letter abbreviation for the local dialect used by the speaker, which is typically the same as the location of the recording, followed by a two-digit number. For example, Istro-Venetian speakers from Pula would be identified as PUL01, PUL02, etc. Consult with one of the principle investigators if you are not sure what code to use.

  • When arranging the time and place for the interview, you should inquire about the availability of a quiet location where you will not be interrupted. See 1.4 below.

1.4 During the interview

  • Choose a quiet location where you can be alone with the participant (if possible). You should avoid recording in a kitchen or outdoors (e.g., on a terrace or balcony). Generally a living room or other space that has curtains, rugs, and upholstered furniture like couches and armchairs is best. If possible, close the windows and turn off all possible sources of noise (air-conditioners, fans, television, telephones). Don’t forget to turn off your own mobile phone!

  • Set up the recorder and position the microphone close to the speaker. Place it on a stable surface – ideally, on a table between you and the participant – and caution the participant not to bump or hit the table during the interview. If possible, remove anything from the table that might make noise (e.g., glasses, cups, ashtrays, etc.). Make a test recording to check the equipment and recording level.

  • When everything is ready, start recording. If possible, do not turn off the recorder until the very end of the interview, after the demographic questionnaire. If you are briefly interrupted, you should leave the recorder on; we can delete these portions of the recording later.

  • State your name, the date, and the name of the city/town/village where the interview is being conducted.

  • Read the oral consent script and obtain the participant’s consent. Fill out the requested information on the oral consent form.

  • Give the participant a copy of the GDPR no signature consent document for their records.

  • Conduct the interview (approximately 1 hour in length). See the sociolinguistic interview guide for suggested questions. You should follow the order of sections in this guide, but you do not have to include every section or every question. You may follow the lead of the participant and spend more time on topics that are of interest to them, but try to include several different topics, and do not focus entirely on the past – be sure to include topics/questions that deal with their current activities and interests. The only obligatory topic is Language (#11), so reserve some time for this at the end of the interview.

  • Complete the demographic questionnaire orally with the participant. Fill in the answers on your copy of the questionnaire.

  • Stop the recording.

  • Thank the participant for their time and ask again if they would like to receive a copy of the interview recording. If so, please send it to them as soon as possible by e-mail, or provide it on a CD or USB memory stick. (You will be reimbursed for any costs for doing this.)

1.5 After the interview

  • Transfer the recording to your computer and name the file, following the conventions in 2.1.

  • Upload the file to the folder “Upload original interview recordings here” in the shared OneDrive folder for the project and notify Keith Langston.

  • Upload a scan of the completed Oral consent form to the folder “Upload consent documents here”

  • Fill out the Google form for the demographic questionnaire.
